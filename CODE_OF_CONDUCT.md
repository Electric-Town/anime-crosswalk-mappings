# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the claim, not the person making it. Assume the person on
the other end knows something you do not, because in a project about reconciling
contradictory catalogue data they very often do.

## What we expect

**Disagree specifically.** "That offset is wrong, here is the page I checked and the date I
checked it" is useful. "That is obviously wrong" is not, and it is the beginning of an
argument nobody enjoys.

**Accept correction without drama.** Everyone who works on this data will publish something
wrong. The person who points it out has done you a favour.

**Give people room to be new.** Not knowing how a cour maps onto a season is a starting
condition, not a character flaw. Someone asking a basic question in an issue is someone
trying to help.

**Respect the boundaries the project sets.** Some sources cannot be used here for licensing
reasons, and those decisions are documented with their reasoning. Argue with the reasoning in
an issue if you think it is wrong. Do not route around it in a pull request.

## What we will not accept

Harassment, personal attacks, or sustained hostility toward anyone. Demeaning comments about
who someone is rather than what they said. Publishing anyone's private information. Deliberate
disruption of issues, reviews, or discussions. Sexual attention or imagery in project spaces.

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, reviews, commit messages, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will remove, edit, or reject contributions that do not meet this standard, and
will say why when they do.

Report a problem through the private route in [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md). Reports go to all
maintainers. If your report concerns a maintainer, say so, and they will not be involved in
handling it.

We will acknowledge a report within seven days. Responses run from a private word through to
a permanent ban, in proportion to what happened and whether it continued after being raised.
The person who reported something will be told what was decided.

We would rather resolve something quietly and keep a contributor than make an example of
anyone. That preference is not unlimited, and repeated or deliberate harm ends participation.

## Scope

This applies in project spaces and when someone is publicly representing the project. It is
not a licence for maintainers to police unrelated conduct elsewhere.
